# v16.04-shared (29 Sep 2026)

CRM lead-source tags, requested by SG: every lead from the website is tagged by how it found us.

## Tags
- **`seoinbound`:** the lead found the site through:
  - a search engine: Google, Bing, Yahoo, DuckDuckGo, Ecosia, Brave, Yandex or Baidu;
  - an AI assistant: ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot or Claude.

  Paid ads (`utm_medium` cpc/ppc/paid) are excluded.
- **`emailinbound`:** the lead clicked a link in one of our emails. That means either:
  - `utm_medium=email` (or `newsletter`) on the link; or
  - the visitor arrived from a webmail site such as Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo Mail or AOL.
- **No tag:** direct visits, social media and other websites get neither tag. They keep the existing `source-…` tag.

## Which forms add the tags
- "List your business": the tag is added next to the existing `source-…` tag.
- **Claim a listing:** the tag is added next to `claim-request`. Claims now also record where the visitor came from, which they didn't before. That is saved in the `claims` columns `utm_*`, `landing_page` and `lead_ip`, plus a `source-…` tag.

## Change to how the source is remembered
- The visitor's source is kept for 90 days.
- A link with `utm_source` or `utm_medium` now replaces an earlier visit's source. So a business that first found us on Google and later clicks our campaign email counts as `emailinbound`.
- **For campaign emails:** add `?utm_source=ghl&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=<name>` to every link. Many email apps hide where a click came from, and without these tags the lead can't be recognised as email.

`leadChannelTag(src, med)` in worker.js holds the rules.
